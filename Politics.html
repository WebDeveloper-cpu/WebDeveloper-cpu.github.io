Opposition Leaders Adebayo, Momodu Challenge Tinubu Administration to Defend Socio-Economic Record

Published on Political Insights | Updates & Analysis
Political commentators and opposition leaders call for accountability on socio-economic governance.

ABUJA — As political realignments intensify ahead of the upcoming 2027 general elections, prominent political commentators and opposition figures have issued direct challenges to President Bola Ahmed Tinubu’s administration, demanding a transparent defense of its socio-economic policies and performance metrics.

Among the leading voices urging national accountability are Prince Adewole Adebayo, the 2023 presidential candidate of the Social Democratic Party (SDP), and Chief Dele Momodu, a veteran journalist, media mogul, and senior chieftain of the People’s Democratic Party (PDP).

Urgent Calls for Socio-Economic Accountability

Speaking during separate public forums and national broadcasts, both opposition leaders stressed that the administration's economic reforms—notably the removal of the petrol subsidy and the unification of the foreign exchange market—have disproportionately impacted average citizens through hyperinflation, high food prices, and reduced purchasing power.

"Government policies must be measured by their outcome on the lives of ordinary citizens, not just macroeconomic projections on paper. The administration needs to defend its track record with measurable improvements in living standards," stated Prince Adewole Adebayo.

Adebayo argued that while reform measures are often presented as painful structural necessities, the government has yet to demonstrate effective mitigation strategies or robust social safety nets to cushion the socio-economic blow on low-income households across the country.

2027 Electoral Strategy and Party Realignment

Echoing similar sentiments, Chief Dele Momodu noted that the upcoming political cycle will be defined by record-based politics rather than regional or ethnic sentiments. Momodu urged opposition parties to unite on policy-driven platforms that prioritize good governance, transparency, and economic revitalization.

Momodu emphasized that political commentary in the current climate must hold incumbent officials accountable, challenging the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC) to present tangible evidence of socio-economic progress prior to seeking renewal of their electoral mandate.

Civil Society and Religious Leaders Join the Call

The push by Adebayo and Momodu coincides with broader declarations from religious groups, civil society organizations, and regional forums—including the Middle Belt Forum and the Catholic Bishops Conference of Nigeria—who have similarly called on federal authorities to implement immediate policy realignments to alleviate widespread economic hardship.

As coalition talks continue to build among key opposition elements, observers expect socio-economic governance and public accountability to remain the dominant battleground issues leading up to the 2027 elections.
